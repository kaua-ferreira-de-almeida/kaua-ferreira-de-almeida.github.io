Robotic 3D reconstruction of a shoe

SIGMA Clermont · Innovative Mechanisms and Robots (IMR) · S7–S8 · 2025–2026

Can a robot scan a flexible object in 3D?

Our eight-student team built a robotic scanning prototype for industrial inspection and remanufacturing. We used a shoe as a test object because its curved surfaces hide parts of the geometry from any single angle. A simulated UR3e arm moved a color-and-depth camera around the shoe so we could reconstruct its shape from several views.

My contribution · Shoe segmentation

I trained YOLOv11 Nano instance segmentation models in Roboflow to identify which pixels belong to the shoe. The dataset shown below contains 1,551 images, including views of different shoes, orientations and lighting conditions. We used the predicted masks to keep the shoe’s color and depth measurements and filter out the background before reconstruction.

From camera views to a 3D model

01

Move around the shoe

We mounted a simulated RGB-D camera on the UR3e wrist in Gazebo. We started with fixed waypoints, then used color and depth to estimate the shoe’s center and generate viewpoints around it. MoveIt 2 planned the motion while the camera stayed pointed at the shoe.

02

Separate the shoe from the scene

A ROS 2 node sent color frames to the model hosted on Roboflow and applied the returned shoe masks to the aligned depth images. We inspected the color images, depth maps, camera frames and point clouds in RViz to check coverage and alignment.

03

Combine the views

ROS 2’s TF tree provided the camera pose at each frame’s timestamp. The Open3D-based pipeline used those poses to fuse the depth measurements in a shared 3D volume (TSDF), then extracted a triangle mesh with Marching Cubes and exported it as a PLY file.

What changed between the two tests

In early scans, the arm moved faster than the cloud segmentation service could process frames. The reconstruction missed some viewpoints and produced gaps and stray geometry. We slowed the simulated arm’s 240° sweep to 110 seconds and limited inference to one frame per second. The later mesh captures the shoe’s overall shape, including its opening and sole, with some residual artifacts around the edges.

The team evaluated the reconstruction qualitatively in simulation. A next step is to run the full pipeline on the physical UR3e and measure accuracy against a reference scan.
